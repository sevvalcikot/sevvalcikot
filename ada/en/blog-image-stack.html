Removing crowds from a photo with Image Stack

I made a busy tram stop look empty using a few photos and Photoshop's Median stack mode.

Categories
  • Photo Editing
  • Graphic Design
Tags
  • #Adobe
  • #Photoshop
  • #image stack
  • #median

Taking an empty photo of the Akdeniz University tram stop is almost impossible; passengers and vehicles pass by all the time. In this project I combined several photos taken from the same angle in Photoshop and removed everything that moved.

The idea

People stand in different places in each photo, but the building, the rails and the ground stay in the same place. When Photoshop looks at every pixel across all the photos and picks the middle (median) value, people who appear in only one or two frames disappear and the still scene remains.

Steps

  1. I held my phone still and took 8–10 photos from the same angle, a few seconds apart.
  2. I opened them as layers in one file with File → Scripts → Load Files into Stack and ticked "Attempt to Automatically Align Source Images".
  3. I selected all layers and chose right-click → Convert to Smart Object.
  4. I went to Layer → Smart Objects → Stack Mode → Median.
  5. I cleaned up the small leftovers with the Spot Healing Brush.

What I learned

The more photos you have, the cleaner the result. Steady light matters too: mixing cloudy and sunny frames left blotches in the image. The same method can also reduce noise in a photo; in that case you choose Mean as the stack mode.
